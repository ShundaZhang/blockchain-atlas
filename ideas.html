01 / FIELD NOTES

Decentralized money relocates authority over issuance

The aspiration is rule-based money without one discretionary issuer.

Bitcoin’s monetary idea challenges dependence on central issuers and payment intermediaries. Participating nodes enforce an issuance schedule and reject invalid blocks, including unauthorized creation of coins. This offers a way to hold and transfer a native asset under public rules, rather than a liability issued by a bank or token company.

“Opposing the dominance of currency issuance” is an ideological position, not a guaranteed technical outcome. Open-source rules can be debated and changed through adoption; mining, custody, software distribution and economic influence can concentrate. Restricting issuance does not guarantee stable purchasing power, and a usable medium of exchange is not automatically a good unit of account.

02 / FIELD NOTES

Compare who sets rules and who can intervene

Decentralization has several dimensions, not one label.

ModelIssuance / backingControl and tradeoff
Sovereign currencyMonetary institutions and banking systemPolicy flexibility and public institutions; dependence on institutional governance
BitcoinNative asset with node-enforced issuance rulesOpen verification and self-custody; volatility, key loss and concentration remain
USD₮ / USDTIssuer-managed token linked to reserve and redemption arrangementsFiat pricing on a blockchain; issuer controls and off-chain dependencies remain

Institutional monetary literature stresses payment reliability and a common unit of account; permissionless-system proponents stress independent verification and censorship resistance. Read these as different explicit priorities. Neither a blockchain label nor a public institution removes the need to inspect actual powers and failure modes.

03 / FIELD NOTES

Web3 proposes user-controlled state and ownership

Inspect the whole application, not only its token.

Web3 commonly describes applications where keys, contracts and open protocols support user-held assets and portable participation. A wallet can authorize an action without creating a platform account; a DAO can coordinate a treasury through proposals and voting. These are design possibilities, not a universal architecture standard.

A dapp can still depend on a centralized website, RPC provider, sequencer, oracle or upgrade key. Token voting can favor large holders, and social decisions often remain off-chain. Ask whether users can independently access, verify, exit and change the system, and who can freeze or upgrade it.
