02 / FIELD NOTES

Bitcoin and Ethereum pursue different design spaces

Neither is merely a newer version of the other.

Bitcoin centers on a monetary ledger using UTXOs, a deliberately constrained scripting system and proof-of-work consensus. Ethereum adds general-purpose EVM execution and an account/state model. Its transition to proof of stake changed consensus without making contract execution disappear.

Later systems explore different validator sets, execution models and scaling methods. Compare their validation, finality, data availability and governance rather than ranking them by transaction throughput alone. Historical success or a token’s popularity does not establish a protocol’s suitability for a particular application.

03 / FIELD NOTES

Separate adoption history from price cycles

Technical progress, speculation and infrastructure maturity are different axes.

Learn why a capability appeared: smart contracts enable programmable assets; stablecoins connect token transfers to a fiat unit; rollups move computation while retaining an L1 verification path. Do not treat a rising asset price as evidence that any of these mechanisms is sound.

Read primary protocol timelines for dates and deployment details. Upgrade names and future plans change. This atlas teaches durable mechanisms and pins code dependencies; it does not maintain market prices or recommend assets to buy.
