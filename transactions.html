01 / FIELD NOTES

A transaction has a lifecycle, not just a hash

Authorization, propagation, inclusion and finality are distinct.

The wallet constructs fields such as chain ID, nonce, destination, value, calldata and fee limits; the user authorizes the request. An RPC endpoint broadcasts it, a producer includes it, and execution produces a receipt. A receipt status reports execution success or failure; finality is a later consensus property.

The nonce orders a sender’s transactions and helps prevent replay in that account sequence. The chain ID distinguishes networks. Replacing a pending transaction, indexing lag and reorganizations can change what a front end sees. Check chain, contract address, receipt and relevant state.

02 / FIELD NOTES

Gas quantity and gas price are different units

A reverted transaction can still consume gas.

Gas units measure execution resources; the price is expressed in wei per gas. For a conventional EIP-1559 transaction, effective price is constrained by the fee cap and base fee plus priority fee. Transaction cost is gas used multiplied by the effective price, not automatically the entire gas limit times the maximum fee.

Hypothetical example: 21,000 gas used at 22 gwei/gas costs 0.000462 ETH. The limit bounds available gas, while maxFeePerGas caps price. These numbers illustrate units only; L2 fees can also include an L1 data component.

03 / FIELD NOTES

A wallet’s displayed balance is not the whole trust model

Self-custody, smart accounts and exchange custody differ.

An exchange balance is the exchange’s account record; a self-custodial key authorizes on-chain transactions; a smart account can add recovery and policy logic. A hardware wallet protects key operations but does not determine whether the transaction destination or contract semantics are appropriate.

Tokens are identified by chain and contract, not just a ticker. Sending USDT on one network to an incompatible route is not equivalent to transferring the same asset on another network. Never interpret a token appearing in a wallet list as proof of issuer identity or market value.
